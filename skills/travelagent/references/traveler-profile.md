# Traveler Profile

This is the standing taste model. It was derived from a 14-day Japan build (July 2026) plus the
corrections the traveler gave during it. **Read the "Transfer rules" section carefully.** The Japan trip
produced concrete artifacts (a Mt. Fuji sports-car drive, softshell-turtle hot pot, raw selvedge
denim, a Hanshin Tigers game). Those were correct *for Japan*. They are examples of underlying
preferences, not a checklist to reproduce in Lisbon or Lima.

---

## 1. Pace and philosophy

| Dimension | Setting |
|---|---|
| Density | High. 3 to 5 real anchors per day plus opportunistic stops. Long walking days are a feature. |
| Energy | Active. Walks the whole neighborhood, climbs the tower, rides the bike, takes the early train. |
| Passive leisure | **Excluded.** No resort decompression, no spa days, no beach lounging, no "afternoon at leisure", no pool time, no sunset cruise. |
| Downtime | Built in as *transit* and *meals*, not as scheduled rest. A late night is fine; an empty afternoon is not. |
| Guided groups | Avoid coach tours and large group tours. Private guides or master-led workshops are fine and often preferred. |
| Hotels | Do not recommend hotels, ryokan, riads, or Airbnbs unless explicitly asked. They book lodging themselves. Neighborhood-level advice on *where to base* is welcome; specific properties are not. |

## 2. History and cultural depth

Wants the *why*, not the *what*. A site entry that says "beautiful 14th-century temple, very popular"
is a failure. The entry must carry the historical argument: who built it, against what, what it
displaced, what it meant, why it survived, what it tells you about the place now.

- Strong pull toward: pre-modern religious architecture, castles and fortifications, imperial and
  colonial power architecture, war and atrocity memory sites, industrial heritage, archaeological
  layers, UNESCO inscriptions (flag them), places where a specific historical decision happened.
- Wants sites organized so the history *sequences*: oldest layer to newest, or the arc of a conflict,
  or a dynasty in order, when geography allows it.
- Will read a 150-word writeup per site. Will not read 500 words of guidebook padding.

## 3. Architecture

Both halves, always:

1. **Modern and contemporary.** Named architects, movements (brutalism, metabolism, modernism,
   critical regionalism), museum and civic commissions, adaptive reuse, notable towers, concrete
   churches, transit infrastructure as architecture.
2. **Classic and vernacular.** Traditional townhouse typologies, timber joinery, courtyard housing,
   market halls, tiled facades, arcades, alley fabric, the pre-war street grid.

Deliverables should include at least one **architectural walking route** per major city: an ordered
sequence of buildings within walking distance, with the architect, year, and what to look at.

## 4. Photography (street and night)

This is a primary planning lens, not a bonus. Optimize sites and timing for a photographer working
handheld in dense environments.

- Wants: neon and signage density, narrow alleys and yokocho-type lanes, night markets, wet streets
  after rain, transit interiors and platforms, elevated vantage points (free observation decks,
  rooftop bars, hillside overlooks, bridges), working markets at dawn, laundry-and-wires residential
  fabric, single-bulb counter restaurants, arcade covered streets, harbors at blue hour.
- Note **golden hour and blue hour times for the actual travel dates** and which anchor faces which
  direction where it matters.
- Flag any site that bans photography, bans tripods, or closes before dark.
- Nightlife districts count as photographic subjects, not just as places to drink.

## 5. Dining (the largest single section, always dual-track)

### Track A: high-impact
Awarded, cult, chef-driven, hard to book. Michelin stars and Bib Gourmand, World's 50 Best and its
regional lists, the local critical canon, the single-counter room with one seating, the
decades-old institution with a queue, the chef who trained somewhere famous and went home.

### Track B: hyper-local understated
The hole-in-the-wall. Counter with eight stools, market stall, workers' lunch canteen, the
third-generation family shop that makes one thing, the place with no English menu and no website.
Track B is **equally weighted with Track A**, not a filler category.

### Dish-led, not just venue-led
Build the list around **regional specialties that are hard or impossible to eat elsewhere**. For each
city, produce a short "dishes to hunt" list, then attach the best venues to each dish. If a dish only
exists in one prefecture, province, or valley, say so and say why.

### Rare, seasonal, unconventional
- **Rare proteins and traditional game.** Where a genuine local tradition exists, seek it: freshwater
  and estuary oddities, offal preparations, game, insects, blood dishes, fermented and aged proteins,
  whole-animal cookery. In Japan this meant suppon (softshell turtle), basashi (horse), bear, badger.
  In another country it will mean something completely different, and in some countries it will mean
  nothing at all. **Do not force this category.** Find the authentic local answer to "what protein or
  preparation can I only eat here", and if the honest answer is "nothing unusual", say that and spend
  the effort elsewhere.
- **Seasonal components pinned to the exact travel dates.** What is at peak in that specific month?
  What is a two-week window? Which traditional eating day falls inside the trip?
- **Avant-garde and unconventional** is welcome when it is rooted, not novelty for its own sake.

### Raw and counter seafood
Loves it. Wants the serious version. **Explicitly rejects the commodified version**: no
conveyor-belt sushi, no tourist-strip ceviche, no cruise-port oyster bar. Generalize the rule: for
any dish the destination is famous for, route around the tourist-trap format and name the format
locals use.

### Reservation backups (added at the traveler's request)
Every dining anchor that needs a reservation must ship with **two alternates in the same
neighborhood cluster and the same price band**, tagged so it is obvious they are substitutes. See
`data-schema.md`, field `tier` (`anchor` / `strong` / `alternate`). Rule of thumb: an evening slot in
the plan is not done until it has three names attached.

### Pricing
Always give a range in **local currency and USD**, per person, and say whether it includes drinks and
service. Bands: budget / mid / splurge, stated explicitly rather than implied by symbols.

## 6. Retail, craft, and heritage goods

Shopping is a first-class axis of this traveler's trips, comparable in weight to dining. It gets its
own document sections and its own Google Maps layers.

### Menswear
- Independent artisanal labels, small-batch production, designers with a textile or craft story.
- **Local heritage textile and dye traditions.** In Japan: indigo (aizome), sashiko stitching,
  selvedge denim, knitwear. Elsewhere the analog is different and must be researched fresh: Harris
  tweed and Donegal wool, Portuguese and Irish linen, Peruvian alpaca and vicuna, Oaxacan natural
  dye and backstrap weaving, Florentine leather, Como and Varanasi silk, Turkish towel weaving,
  Scottish knitwear, Uzbek ikat, West African indigo and adire, Danish and Swedish workwear mills.
- Workwear and Americana lineage, and the local equivalent of a workwear canon (French chore coats,
  German Zunftkleidung, Italian moleskin, Japanese noragi).
- **Silhouette preference: wide-leg, high-waisted, pleated trousers.** Textured fabrics and knits.
  Bohemian, unusual, characterful over sleek minimalism. They are not shopping for slim-fit basics.
- Reference brands for calibration: Batoner, Beams Plus, Comoli, Kapital (incl. Century Denim).
  Home boutique reference: **Corridor** (Brooklyn). Use these to gauge *taste level and price
  tolerance*, not as brands to find in every country.
- Also wants: concept stores and multi-brand curators, factory shops and mill outlets, secondhand and
  vintage archive shops, tailors and menders where relevant.

### Non-clothing
| Category | What they want |
|---|---|
| Kitchen cutlery | Hand-forged knives, named smiths, the local blade tradition, sharpening service and lessons |
| Ceramics | Studio pottery, regional kiln traditions, functional ware, contemporary makers |
| Regional craft goods | Lacquer, basketry, paper, glass, metalwork, textiles as objects, anything with a named workshop behind it |
| Vintage watches | **Budget-conscious: hundreds to low thousands USD, not five figures.** Enthusiast shops, flea and antique markets, grey-market dealers. Do not send them to a Patek boutique. |
| Cameras and lenses | Used and vintage market, film bodies, manual glass, local specialist dealers. |

### Retail logistics to include
Opening hours (many small shops close one weekday and open late), tax-free / VAT-refund mechanics
and thresholds, whether they ship internationally, whether appointment is needed, cash-only status,
and knife-in-luggage rules where relevant.

## 7. Craft participation and non-commodified culture

Wants to *do* things with practitioners, not watch a demonstration staged for tour buses.

- Master-led workshops: dyeing, weaving, ceramics, knife making or sharpening, paper, lacquer,
  cooking with a specialist, printmaking.
- Ritual and ceremony in its real form. In Japan this was tea ceremony at a small school rather than
  a hotel show; elsewhere it is whatever the analog is.
- Ask the filter question for every "cultural experience": would a local do this, or is it only sold
  to visitors? If the latter, cut it or say plainly that it is a staged version and why it still earns
  its slot.

## 8. Active and outdoors

- **Cycling** on dedicated infrastructure: urban protected networks, riverside and canal paths,
  coastal routes, island and causeway routes, converted rail trails, mountain descents. Include rental
  logistics (where, cost, e-bike availability, one-way drop-off, whether a passport or deposit is
  needed).
- **Driving routes** only where the destination genuinely has them: a real road culture, scenic roads
  worth the day, and a rental market. Include the full logistics chain: license and **International
  Driving Permit** requirements (which convention, where to obtain, lead time), insurance, tolls,
  parking, fuel, one-way fees, and any seasonal road closures. The Japan build included a classic and
  performance-car rental day; that was appropriate *because Japan has that scene*. Do not clone a
  "sports car day" into a country where it makes no sense.
- Hiking, coastal walks, volcano and ridge routes, urban stair walks, swimming where it is a local
  practice rather than a resort activity.

## 9. Live spectator sport

Wants **one live local sporting event per trip for the crowd culture**, not because they follow the
sport. Pick the event where the stadium ritual is the point: Japanese and Korean baseball, sumo,
European and South American football, Gaelic games, cricket in South Asia, basketball in the
Philippines, lucha libre in Mexico, bullfighting-adjacent traditions where they would want the
cultural context and the ethics flagged. Include: schedule for the actual dates, how tickets are
sold, whether foreign cards work, section choice for atmosphere, and stadium food to eat.

## 10. Hard requirements the traveler has stated

These are non-negotiable output rules, restated in `deliverables-spec.md` and enforced in the
build scripts.

1. **Reservation flag on everything that needs one**, with lead time band: months out / weeks out /
   a few days / same day / walk-in. Applies to restaurants, museums, monuments, permits, workshops,
   sporting events, and transport.
2. **Closure awareness.** Flag weekly closed days, closure on specific dates inside the travel
   window, holiday closures, and seasonal closures. Check the actual trip dates against them.
3. **Operating hours wherever a place is mentioned**, in the compact notation, including in the
   Google Maps export note field.
4. **Word format** with bullets and sub-bullets, a one-page summary before the detail, links, and
   price ranges in local currency and USD.
5. **Google Maps CSV exports**, split so different types get different icons.
6. **Clustering blocks** (neighborhood clusters and day sequencing) are a liked feature. Keep them.
7. **No em dashes. No emojis in prose. No AI-slop phrasing.** Direct, dense, active voice.
8. Full literal file paths in any report of where output landed.

## Transfer rules: what generalizes and what does not

| Japan artifact | Do NOT copy | The underlying preference to re-derive |
|---|---|---|
| Mt. Fuji classic-car drive | A "sports car day" anywhere | Is there a real driving culture and a scenic-road payoff here? If not, spend the day on cycling, rail, or walking. |
| Suppon, basashi, bear, badger | Hunting for turtle in Portugal | What protein or preparation can they only eat *here*, in a genuine tradition? |
| Raw selvedge denim, aizome indigo, sashiko | Looking for denim mills in Peru | What is the local textile and dye craft, and who are its living makers and shops? |
| Hanshin Tigers at Koshien | Baseball everywhere | What is the local spectator sport with the best crowd ritual during these dates? |
| Tabelog, OMAKASE, Pocket Concierge | Assuming these exist | What is the local booking stack, and does it require a local phone or card? |
| Tea ceremony | Tea ceremony in Italy | What is the local ritual practice worth participating in? |
| Kyoto machiya, Tokyo yokocho | Same vocabulary elsewhere | What is the local dense-fabric typology worth photographing? |
| Tokyo-heavy 60/25/15 city split | Reusing that ratio | Derive the split from the destination's actual density of *their* interests. |
| July hamo, doyo-no-ushi-no-hi | Japanese seasonality | What is in season and what traditional eating days fall inside the dates? |

**The invariants** (carry these to every destination): high-density active pace; deep historical
argument per site; dual modern-and-classic architecture with a walking route; street and night
photography optimization; dual-track dining with dish-led regional hunting and rare-protein
curiosity; heritage textile and craft retail as a major axis with wide-leg pleated silhouette
preference; hand-forged blades, ceramics, affordable vintage watches, used cameras; master-led
workshops over demonstrations; cycling on real infrastructure; one live local sporting event;
reservations, hours, and closures flagged throughout; no passive leisure; no hotels.
